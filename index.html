Why economics

Like most Argentines, I grew up with the news on: inflation at 200%, the dollar in every conversation, one crisis after another. At some point the headlines weren't enough. I wanted to know why this keeps happening.

That curiosity is what got me into economics. What I found at university wasn't a recipe for fixing Argentina. It was something more useful: a way to tell which questions are the right ones, and the tools to test them against real data. The answers are hard, because every country's problems are its own. But asking the right question is where it starts, and that's what my projects try to do.

Questions I keep coming back to

  • What happened to real wages after the December 2023 devaluation? Explored in Real wages in Argentina, a time series model in Stata.
  • Which sectors take the hardest hit in a crisis, and how long do they take to recover? Explored in Sector shocks in economic activity, with Python and Power BI.
  • Why does Argentina keep coming back to high inflation? Next on my list.
  • How much of a devaluation ends up in prices, and how fast? Next on my list.
  • Why does the economy grow in stop-and-go cycles? Next on my list.

I've also worked on business analytics with SQL and Power BI. Everything, with code and data, is in my portfolio.
